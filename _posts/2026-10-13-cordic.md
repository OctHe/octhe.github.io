> **Notation**
>
> | Symbol | Meaning |
> |:---:|:---|
> | $$x_i, y_i$$ | the coordinates after step $$i$$; scalars are in ordinary italics |
> | $$\hat\alpha_i$$ | the angle estimate held by the accumulator after step $$i$$, starting from $$\hat\alpha_0=0$$ |
> | $$\alpha$$ | the true angle of the input vector, $$\alpha=\arctan(y_0/x_0)$$, in radians |

CORDIC (**CO**ordinate **R**otation **DI**gital **C**omputer) is an iterative method that recovers the angle and amplitude of a vector based on shifts and additions.
Without loss of generality, we define

$$
\arctan\frac{y_0}{x_0} \in \left[0,\ \frac{\pi}{2} \right), \qquad x_0>0, \qquad y_0\ge0
$$

The idea behind CORDIC is to rotate the vector onto the real axis with a sequence of fixed angles:

$$
\theta_i=\arctan(2^{-i}), \qquad i=0,1,2,\dots
$$

Note that the tangent of $$\theta_i$$ is $$2^{-i}$$, so shifting can be used instead of multiplication.

# 2. The iteration

## 2.1 The recurrence

Rotating the vector by $$\theta_i$$, we obtain

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x_{i+1} \\ y_{i+1} \end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_i & -\sin\theta_i \\ \sin\theta_i & \cos\theta_i \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x_i \\ y_i \end{bmatrix}
= \cos\theta_i \begin{bmatrix} x_i - \sigma_i 2^{-i} y_i \\ \sigma_i 2^{-i} x_i + y_i \end{bmatrix}
$$

where

$$
\sigma_i =
\begin{cases}
-1, & y_i\ge0 \\
+1, & y_i<0
\end{cases}
$$

selects the direction.

The iteration process of the angle is

$$
\begin{cases}
x_{i+1} = x_i - \sigma_i 2^{-i} y_i \\
y_{i+1} = y_i + \sigma_i 2^{-i} x_i \\
\hat\alpha_{i+1} = \hat\alpha_i - \sigma_i \theta_i
\end{cases}
$$

where $$\hat\alpha_{0} = 0$$.

The factor for one step is

$$
\cos\theta_i = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\tan^2\theta_i}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+2^{-2i}}}
$$

The factor $$\cos\theta_i$$ is discarded at every step.
Hence, the gain after $$n$$ steps is

$$
K_n = \prod_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\cos\theta_i}
$$


After $$n$$ steps the nominal vector is therefore the input rotated through the accumulated angle and scaled by that gain:

$$
\begin{bmatrix} x_n \\ y_n \end{bmatrix}
= K_n \begin{bmatrix} \cos\Phi & -\sin\Phi \\ \sin\Phi & \cos\Phi \end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} x_0 \\ y_0 \end{bmatrix},
\qquad \Phi=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\sigma_i\theta_i
$$

where $$\Phi$$ is the total angle actually turned.
The limit of the gain is

$$
K_\infty = \prod_{i\ge0}\sqrt{1+2^{-2i}} = 1.6467602581\ldots
$$
