# 1. System model

Wireless system model:

$$
\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{H} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{n}, \qquad
\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_r \times N_t}, \quad
\mathbf{n} \sim \mathcal{CN}(\mathbf{0}, \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})
$$

The MMSE solution is

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{W} \mathbf{y}, \qquad
\mathbf{W} = (\mathbf{H}^H \mathbf{H} + \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{H}^H = \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{H}^H
$$

where $$\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{H}^H \mathbf{H} + \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}$$.

# 2. QR-based MMSE with Extended Channel Matrix

The extended channel and received signal are

$$
\bar{\mathbf{H}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{H} \\ \sigma \mathbf{I}_{N_t} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad
\bar{\mathbf{y}} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{y} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{bmatrix}
$$

The QR decomposition of $$\bar{\mathbf{H}}$$ is

$$
\bar{\mathbf{H}} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{R}, \qquad
\mathbf{Q} = \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{Q}_1 \\ \mathbf{Q}_2 \end{bmatrix}
$$

where $$\mathbf{Q}_1 \in \mathbb{C}^{N_r \times N_t}$$ is the signal (channel) block, $$\mathbf{Q}_2 \in \mathbb{C}^{N_t \times N_t}$$ the noise (regularization) block, and $$\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_t \times N_t}$$ is upper triangular.

Therefore, the inverse of $$\mathbf{R}$$ comes for free from the noise block.

$$
\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{Q}_1 \mathbf{R}
$$

$$
 \sigma \mathbf{I} = \mathbf{Q}_2 \mathbf{R} \rightarrow \mathbf{R}^{-1} = \frac{1}{\sigma}\,\mathbf{Q}_2
$$


The MMSE weight matrix is built from the two blocks $$\mathbf{Q}_1$$ and $$\mathbf{Q}_2$$ by

$$
\mathbf{W} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{H}^H
= \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{R}^{-H} \mathbf{R}^H \mathbf{Q}_1^H
= \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{Q}_1^H
= \frac{1}{\sigma} \mathbf{Q}_2 \mathbf{Q}_1^H
$$


## 2.1 Bias

The MMSE estimate is biased, which causes the estimated value to be smaller than the true value.

**Bias from the extended QR.**

$$
\mathbf{y}_r = \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{y} = \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{H} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{n}
= \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{Q}_1 \mathbf{R} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{n}
= (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q}_2^H \mathbf{Q}_2) \mathbf{R} \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{Q}_1^H \mathbf{n}
$$

Back-substitution $$\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{y}_r$$ treats $$\mathbf{R}$$ as the channel, whereas the signal is in fact multiplied by $$(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q}_2^H \mathbf{Q}_2) \mathbf{R}$$.

**Bias from the weight matrix.**
The weight matrix obeys

$$
\mathbf{W} \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} \mathbf{H}^H \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{A}^{-1} (\mathbf{A} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{I}) = \mathbf{I} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{A}^{-1}
$$

so the estimate splits into a signal part and a noise part,

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}} = \mathbf{W} \mathbf{y} = (\mathbf{I} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{A}^{-1}) \mathbf{x} + \mathbf{W} \mathbf{n}
$$

The diagonal elements of $$(\mathbf{I} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{A}^{-1})$$ are all strictly less than 1, since $$\sigma^2 \mathbf{A}^{-1}$$ is positive definite.

The two biases are the same:

$$
\mathbf{R}^{-1} (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{Q}_2^H \mathbf{Q}_2) \mathbf{R}
= \mathbf{I} - \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{Q}_2^H \mathbf{Q}_2 \mathbf{R}
= \mathbf{I} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{R}^{-H}
= \mathbf{I} - \sigma^2 \mathbf{A}^{-1}
= \mathbf{W} \mathbf{H}
$$


# 3. QR-based MMSE with $$\mathbf{H}$$

The QR decomposition is

$$
\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{Q} \mathbf{R}, \qquad \mathbf{Q}^H \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{I}_{N_t}
$$

where $$\mathbf{Q} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_r \times N_t}$$ and upper triangular $$\mathbf{R} \in \mathbb{C}^{N_t \times N_t}$$.

The estimated signal is

$$
\hat{\mathbf{x}} = (\mathbf{R}^H \mathbf{R} + \sigma^2 \mathbf{I})^{-1} \mathbf{R}^H \mathbf{y}_r
$$

where $$\mathbf{y}_r = \mathbf{Q}^H \mathbf{y}$$ is the rotated received signal.

This approach is the basis of nonlinear receivers, but it has no advantages for MMSE.

# 4. Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)


$$
\mathrm{SNR}_k = \frac{1}{\sigma^2 (\mathbf{A}^{-1})_{kk}} - 1
$$

With the extended QR decomposition, we have

$$
(\mathbf{A}^{-1})_{kk} = \lVert (\mathbf{R}^{-1})_{k,:} \rVert^2
$$

$$
(\mathbf{A}^{-1})_{kk} = \frac{1}{\sigma^2} \lVert (\mathbf{Q}_2)_{k,:} \rVert^2
$$

Therefore,

$$
\mathrm{SNR}_k = \frac{1}{\lVert (\mathbf{Q}_2)_{k,:} \rVert^2} - 1
$$
